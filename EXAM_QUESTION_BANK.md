## Section A — Objective practice (30 questions)
Choose one best answer.

1. Which organelle is the principal site of aerobic respiration in eukaryotic cells? A. Ribosome B. Mitochondrion C. Golgi apparatus D. Lysosome
2. Osmosis is the net movement of: A. glucose through any membrane B. water through a selectively permeable membrane down its water-potential gradient C. oxygen against its gradient using ATP D. proteins through cell walls
3. Which reagent is used to test a sample for starch? A. Iodine solution B. Benedict's solution C. Biuret reagent D. Limewater
4. Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart? A. Pulmonary artery B. Vena cava C. Pulmonary vein D. Aorta
5. The functional unit of the kidney is the: A. alveolus B. nephron C. villus D. neuron
6. Which process produces genetically varied haploid cells? A. Mitosis B. Meiosis C. Binary fission only D. Budding only
7. The main role of chlorophyll is to: A. absorb light energy B. absorb glucose from soil C. transport sugar in phloem D. digest proteins
8. Which is a density-dependent limiting factor? A. Competition for food B. A meteor strike C. A solar eclipse D. A volcanic eruption regardless of population density
9. Which group is acellular and replicates using host-cell machinery? A. Fungi B. Bacteria C. Viruses D. Protozoa
10. Which molecule is the immediate energy-transfer currency commonly used by cells? A. ATP B. DNA C. Cellulose D. Bile
11. In a simple cross Aa × Aa, the probability of aa offspring is: A. 1/4 B. 1/2 C. 3/4 D. 1
12. Which structure increases the absorptive surface of the small intestine? A. Villi B. Tracheal rings C. Alveolar cartilage D. Heart valves
13. Which hormone generally increases water reabsorption in collecting ducts? A. ADH B. Insulin C. Adrenaline D. Oestrogen
14. Which is a producer in a grazing food chain? A. Green grass B. Grasshopper C. Frog D. Hawk
15. Which enzyme catalyses the breakdown of starch? A. Amylase B. Lipase C. Pepsin D. Catalase only
16. Which process removes metabolic urea from the body? A. Excretion B. Egestion C. Ingestion D. Pollination
17. Which statement about natural selection is most accurate? A. Individuals choose useful mutations B. Heritable variants can leave different numbers of offspring C. Every organism evolves during its lifetime D. Selection always makes organisms more complex
18. Which cell structure is the main site of protein synthesis? A. Ribosome B. Cell wall C. Vacuole D. Centriole only
19. In gel electrophoresis, DNA fragments generally migrate toward the: A. positive electrode B. negative electrode C. centre and stop D. nearest water source
20. Which test commonly gives a violet/lilac result when protein is present? A. Biuret test B. Iodine test C. Benedict's test D. Ethanol emulsion test
21. Which ecological method is suitable for estimating the density of stationary plants? A. Quadrat sampling B. Pulse measurement C. Spirometry D. Blood typing
22. Which gas is released as a product of photosynthesis? A. Oxygen B. Nitrogen C. Methane D. Helium
23. What is the main role of platelets? A. Blood clotting B. Oxygen transport C. Antibody secretion only D. Bile production
24. Which organ produces bile? A. Liver B. Kidney C. Spleen D. Urinary bladder
25. The hereditary material in most organisms is: A. DNA B. starch C. cellulose D. triglyceride
26. Which process converts atmospheric carbon dioxide into organic molecules in green plants? A. Photosynthesis B. Combustion C. Egestion D. Nitrification only
27. A population consists of: A. individuals of the same species in an area B. every species on Earth C. all abiotic factors only D. one organism and all its organs
28. Which is an example of ex-situ conservation? A. A seed bank B. A protected natural forest only C. A marine reserve in its natural ecosystem D. Protecting a wild population in place
29. During transcription, a cell synthesises: A. RNA using a DNA template B. DNA from a protein template C. starch from amino acids D. lipid from ribosomes only
30. Why are controls used in an experiment? A. To provide a comparison for interpreting results B. To guarantee the hypothesis is correct C. To replace repeated measurements D. To remove the need for recording observations

## Section B — WAEC/NECO-style structured practice

These are original practice prompts. Marking points below are guides, not official examiner schemes.

### 1. Cell structure (10 marks)
(a) Draw and label a typical plant cell. (5)
(b) State two functions of the cell membrane and two functions of the cell wall. (4)
(c) Name one structure found in many plant cells but absent from typical animal cells. (1)

**Suggested marking points:** large clear biological drawing with title; labels such as cell wall, membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus, vacuole and chloroplast where appropriate; membrane selectively controls movement; wall supports/protects; chloroplast is present in many photosynthetic plant cells. Labels must point accurately to the structures.

### 2. Photosynthesis experiment (8 marks)
Describe how you would test whether a leaf exposed to light has formed starch. State the positive result and one safety precaution.

**Suggested marking points:** destarch plant first; expose leaf to light; boil leaf in water; heat leaf in ethanol using a water bath to remove chlorophyll; rinse/soften leaf; add iodine; blue-black indicates starch; keep ethanol away from direct flame and use eye protection/supervision.

### 3. Human circulation (8 marks)
(a) Name the four chambers of the human heart. (4)
(b) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than that of the right ventricle. (2)
(c) State two functions of blood. (2)

**Suggested marking points:** right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle; left ventricle pumps through systemic circulation at higher pressure; blood transports gases/nutrients/hormones/wastes and supports defence, clotting and temperature regulation.

### 4. Genetics (10 marks)
In a plant, tallness (T) is dominant to shortness (t). Cross two heterozygous plants. Show the gametes, offspring genotypes, genotype ratio and phenotype ratio.

**Suggested answer:** Parents Tt × Tt; gametes T and t from each parent; offspring TT, Tt, Tt, tt; genotype ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt; phenotype ratio 3 tall : 1 short, assuming complete dominance and equal viability.

### 5. Ecology and sampling (8 marks)
Explain how a student could use quadrats to estimate the abundance of a plant species in a school field. Give two precautions that improve reliability.

**Suggested marking points:** define the field boundary; choose quadrat size; place quadrats randomly or systematically with a stated rationale; count the target species or estimate cover; repeat many times; calculate mean density/cover; use the same counting rules and avoid choosing only areas where the plant is common.

### 6. Excretion (8 marks)
(a) Define excretion. (2)
(b) Describe two roles of the kidney. (4)
(c) Distinguish excretion from egestion. (2)

**Suggested marking points:** removal of metabolic wastes; filtration of blood, selective reabsorption, secretion, water/ion balance and urine formation; egestion is removal of undigested food from the digestive tract.

### 7. Evolution (6 marks)
Explain how natural selection may increase the frequency of an inherited characteristic in a population.

**Suggested marking points:** variation exists; some variation is inherited; environmental conditions create differential survival/reproduction; individuals with the advantageous heritable trait leave more offspring on average; associated alleles become more common over generations.

### 8. Microorganisms and disease (6 marks)
State three ways communicable diseases can spread and one prevention method for each.

**Suggested marking points:** contaminated food/water—safe water and food hygiene; droplets/air—ventilation and appropriate respiratory precautions; vector—mosquito nets/vector control; blood—sterile equipment; sexual contact—barrier protection and testing. Award only scientifically suitable pairings.

## Section C — Practical/data interpretation practice

### 1. Food test results
A student obtained the following results: sample A turned iodine blue-black; sample B formed an orange precipitate after heating with Benedict's solution; sample C turned violet with Biuret reagent.
- Identify the nutrient indicated in each sample.
- State one limitation of interpreting a negative result.

**Guide:** A contains starch; B contains a reducing sugar; C contains protein. A negative result means the substance was not detected under the test conditions, not necessarily that absolutely none exists.

### 2. Quadrat data
Five quadrats each covered 1 m². Counts of a plant were 4, 6, 5, 3 and 7.
- Calculate the mean number per m².
- State why one sample alone may be unreliable.

**Guide:** Total = 25; mean = 25 ÷ 5 = 5 plants per m². Counts can vary spatially, and more representative samples reduce uncertainty.

### 3. Enzyme investigation
Plan an experiment to investigate the effect of temperature on amylase activity.

**Guide:** identify temperature as independent variable and time to endpoint or amount of starch remaining as dependent variable; use equal enzyme/substrate volumes and concentrations; use buffers and a controlled pH; equilibrate tubes at set temperatures; test at consistent intervals with iodine or use a validated quantitative method; repeat trials; include suitable controls; record a table and graph; observe safe handling instructions.

### 4. Biological drawing checklist
For a specimen drawing, use a clear large outline, a title, accurate proportions, single clean lines, horizontal ruled labels that do not cross, and no shading unless specifically required. Draw what is observed rather than adding structures that cannot be seen.
